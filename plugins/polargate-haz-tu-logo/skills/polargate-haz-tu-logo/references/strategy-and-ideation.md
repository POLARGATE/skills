# Strategy and ideation: where world-class marks actually come from

The shape is the last 10 percent. The idea is the rest. A mark wins because it carries a true, specific, ownable idea that competitors cannot reuse. This file is the engine for finding that idea. Read it in phases 0 and 1.

## 1. Positioning front-end

Before any idea, get crisp on five things. If the user gave them, restate in one block and move on. If not, ask once, focused.

- Essence: what the brand is and who it serves, in one sentence.
- Three attributes: the personality, as adjectives (e.g. precise, kinetic, premium). These steer everything.
- Category and incumbents: what the sector's logos default to, and 1 to 2 specific competitors to look unlike.
- Contexts: where the mark lives (app tile, favicon, signage, embroidery, dark UI, print). This sets the simplicity ceiling.
- Constraints: existing colors, names, must-avoids, casing of the name.

A logo does not need to explain the business. It needs to be a distinctive container the brand fills with meaning over time. Resist the urge to depict what the company does; aim for ownable, not literal.

## 2. What the mark must signal

Write one line: "This mark must feel [attribute] and read unlike [category default], used mainly at [context]." This is the north star for the critique gate. Everything that does not serve it is decoration.

## 3. The idea-generation engine

Generate ideas before geometry. Run several of these in parallel, out loud, briefly:

- Attribute mapping: for each of the three attributes, list the forms, motions, and structures that embody it (precise to grids, right angles, exact arcs; kinetic to diagonal, offset, implied motion; premium to restraint, generous space, fine weight). Look for forms that satisfy two attributes at once, those are the strong ones.
- Asset inventory: the name's initials, a meaningful letter, a number, a structural truth of the product (a layer, a node, a path, a cut), a founding idea. These are raw material, not yet a mark.
- Property over picture: instead of drawing the thing, encode a property of it (ascent, connection, aperture, flow, balance, momentum) as abstract geometry. This is where ownable abstract marks come from.

## 4. Metaphor laddering

Take a concept and climb from literal to abstract. Literal is cliché, abstract is ownable.

- Rung 1, literal: the obvious object (a globe for global). Reject, this is the cliché floor.
- Rung 2, associative: a related object or action (orbit, a path, a horizon). Better, still common.
- Rung 3, abstract: the underlying property as pure form (a continuous line that returns, an arc that implies a larger whole). This is the target.

Always push at least to rung 3 before committing. The cliché blocklist in `references/construction.md` is the list of rung-1 traps.

## 5. Concept-smashing

The most memorable marks fuse two ideas into one form so the second meaning is a discovery (the classic arrow hidden between two letters). Method: list the brand's two strongest concepts, then look for a single shape that is honestly both at once. It must read as the primary form first and reveal the second on a second look. If the viewer has to be told the hidden meaning, drop it. Forced double meanings are worse than one clean idea.

## 6. Letterform thinking

For lettermarks and monograms, the letters are the playground:

- Build initials on a shared geometric system so they feel like one unit, not two glyphs side by side.
- Find the overlap, ligature, or shared stroke that lets two letters become one mark.
- Modify a single letter so it carries the idea (a cut, an extension, a counter that becomes a symbol).
- Balance counters (enclosed white space) by eye, not by math.

For wordmarks, the idea often lives in one customized letter, not in restyling all of them. See `references/typography.md`.

## 7. Gestalt-driven negative space

The eye completes shapes (closure), groups near things (proximity), follows lines (continuity), and separates figure from ground. Use these deliberately:

- Closure: imply a form with gaps the eye closes. Reads as confident and modern.
- Figure-ground: design the white as carefully as the black; the counter can be the mark.
- Continuity: a single path the eye follows feels resolved and intentional.

Negative space is a tool, not a goal. Use it when the hidden form is true to the brand and reads without a caption.

## 8. Articulating and pressure-testing the idea

For each surviving direction, write the idea in one sentence and pressure-test it:

- Is it true to an attribute, not generic?
- Is it unlike the category's defaults?
- Could a competitor put their name on it unchanged? If yes, it is not ownable, kill it.
- Does it survive reduction to one flat color at 16px?

Only ideas that pass become geometry. This is the difference between a logo and a clip-art symbol.
